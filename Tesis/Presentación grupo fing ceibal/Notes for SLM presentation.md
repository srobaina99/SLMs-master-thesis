- Llama architecture
- 3 epochs of 1T tokens of training data
	- Mix of language and code related data sourced from the Slimpajama and StarCoder datasets

	- Rotary Positional Embedding
	- RMSNorm input normalization
	- SwiGLU, a combination of Swish and Gated Linear Unit

		- grouped-query attention
		- dividing key-value heads into groups
	- Others...
- **Come close to o beats Llama2** on some benchmarks, lagging considerably on other

## Phi 
#### Phi-1 (1.3B) 
- Focused on code generation
- 7B tokens:
	- filtered code-language dataset from stack and stackoverflow

- Outperformed much larger models such as the Codex-12B (trained with 100B tokens), CodeGen-Mono-16.1B (577B tokens), PaLM-Coder-540B (780B tokens), and GPT3.5 175B (dataset size unknown)
#### Phi-1.5 (1.3B)

#### Phi-2 (2.7B)
- Embedded phi1.5 knowledge
- close to the Llama-2 70B when tested across common sense reasoning, language understanding, math and coding tasks
#### Phi-3 (2.8B - 14B)
- The Phi3-mini, a 3.8 billion parameter language model, competes with larger models like Mixtral 8x7B and GPT-3.5

# Approaches to create SLMs

## Training
### Imitation
Imitate bigger LLM
- Relies on the quality and diversity of the demonstration data
- Presupposes the availability of high-quality, representative examples, which is not always feasible, particularly in complex or novel scenarios where expert demonstrations are scarce or costly to obtain
- Overfitted to the training data. Can't generalize to new scenarios
### Progressive Learning
**Teacher - student system**, the teacher is prompted to explain its reasoning in every step. It learns the progression
Learns from rich explanation traces signal.
These signals are obtained through system instructions crafted to elicit detailed explanations from a teacher model as it reasons through a task
Presenting multiple solution strategies for different tasks.
Learning from step by step explanations (AI or human) looks promising
#### Example
- Orca, Orca2

### Knowledge Distillation (KD)
Efficient
two-step training process: 
1. Extracting rationales from LLMs: Using techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to elicit detailed rationales from LLMs, which justify their predicted labels. 
2. Training Smaller Models with Rationales: Utilizing these rationales to train smaller task-specific models, focusing on both label prediction and rationale prediction
### Distillation
Basically involves getting a large model showcase a certain type of response that we'd like the small model to have and then.
1. Reasoning Distillation
2. Chain-of-Thought Knowledge Distillation
3. Symbolic Chain-of-Thought Distillation [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.14050v2)

## Modularized training
Ensembles of small LMs and mixture of experts
### Blended ensembles
Combining the responses from multiple smaller models to create collective output
Smaller models might be specialized in a subset of the tasks or subset of data

- A/B testing on the Chai research platform revealed that the blended ensemble not only had higher engagement than each of the constituent systems but also outperformed the larger GPT-3.5 model in terms of user engagement and retention.
#### Mixtral of experts
Each layer of the transformer consists of 8 ff blocks (experts) and a router to choose two of the experts for each token processed.
